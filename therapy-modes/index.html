Therapy Modes

Three distinct electrostimulation modalities, each engineered for specific clinical outcomes.

MICRO — Microcurrent Therapy

Microcurrent therapy delivers electrical current at sub-sensory levels, typically in the microampere range. These currents mirror the body's own bioelectric signals, promoting natural healing processes without patient discomfort.

Clinical Applications:

  • Tissue Healing — Accelerates cellular repair by increasing ATP production and protein synthesis at the treatment site.
  • Edema Reduction — Facilitates lymphatic drainage and reduces inflammation through improved microcirculation.
  • Pain Management — Modulates pain at the cellular level, providing relief for conditions where higher-intensity stimulation may not be appropriate.
  • Wound Healing — Supports faster wound closure and tissue regeneration by enhancing the body's natural electrical repair mechanisms.
  • Fracture Recovery — Supports bone healing processes through bioelectric current application.

TENS — Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation

TENS therapy delivers controlled electrical impulses through the skin to stimulate peripheral nerves. Based on the gate control theory of pain, TENS modulates pain signal transmission to provide effective, drug-free pain relief.

Clinical Applications:

  • Acute Pain — Post-surgical pain, acute injuries, and procedural discomfort management.
  • Chronic Pain — Long-term management of conditions such as arthritis, fibromyalgia, and chronic back pain.
  • Neuropathic Pain — Peripheral neuropathy, diabetic neuropathy, and nerve-related pain conditions.
  • Musculoskeletal Pain — Joint pain, tendinitis, bursitis, and myofascial pain syndromes.
  • Headache Management — Tension headaches and cervicogenic headache conditions.

NMES — Neuromuscular Electrical Stimulation

NMES activates motor neurons to produce controlled muscle contractions, effectively exercising muscles that may be weakened, atrophied, or difficult to activate voluntarily. This modality is essential for rehabilitation and muscle re-education.

Clinical Applications:

  • Muscle Rehabilitation — Restoring strength and function following orthopedic surgery, injury, or prolonged immobilization.
  • Atrophy Prevention — Maintaining muscle mass and function during periods of reduced mobility or post-surgical recovery.
  • Muscle Re-education — Retraining proper muscle activation patterns following neurological events or prolonged disuse.
  • Range of Motion — Improving joint mobility through controlled muscle activation and relaxation cycles.
  • Spasticity Management — Reducing muscle spasticity through reciprocal inhibition and fatigue-based relaxation techniques.
